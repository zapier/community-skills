# Review standards

Use these defaults unless the user supplies applicable standards. User-defined style choices can replace or extend these defaults, but never manufacture evidence or represent unverified assertions as verified. Judge each rule in the context of the reader, purpose, format, and available evidence. A stylistic preference is not automatically a blocker.

## 1. Purpose and angle

Identify the reader's question and the outcome the piece should produce. Check whether the angle is coherent, distinctive enough for its purpose, and matched to the owner's stated objectives. A commercial action is not required for pure analysis or thought leadership. If there is a call to action, it should fit the reader's authority and where the piece appears. Do not infer organizational strategy or penalize a draft for failing an unstated objective.

## 2. Audience and context

Choose one primary reader. Separate someone who executes from someone who recommends, approves, or funds a decision. A title is a clue, not proof of authority. Check the reader's knowledge, likely concerns, and use of the piece. Avoid optimizing contradictory goals for multiple audiences without acknowledging the trade-off.

Before reviewing, define a short audience bar: **register**, **proof**, **travel test**, and **main risk**. Choose the proof and travel criteria based on the reader and format, not an automatic template. Examples are starting points, not fixed rules:

| Reader or use | Useful proof | Appropriate travel test |
|---|---|---|
| Practitioner using a guide | Tested steps or a concrete example | Can they follow the instructions and reach the intended result? |
| Functional leader making a case | Relevant evidence and practical trade-offs | Can they pass a clear recommendation to the person who decides? |
| Decision-maker | Evidence tied to the decision and consequences | Can they understand the choice and direct a next step? |
| Research or report reader | Sources and methodology proportionate to claims | Can a selected finding be understood without missing context? |

If none fits, define a bar from the available evidence. A simulated reader is a hypothesis, never research with actual users.

## 3. Structure and usefulness

Make the central point easy to locate. Check whether sections have distinct jobs, follow a useful order, and deliver the promised payoff. When a piece recommends an action, check whether it includes enough mechanism or example for the intended reader. Thought leadership may legitimately provide a point of view rather than a template. Tutorials may need setup before payoff. Headings should help the specific format: finding-led headings can suit independently shared report sections, while task labels can suit a tutorial. Do not impose arbitrary paragraph limits or a single heading style.

## 4. Voice and surface

Assess consistency, clarity, jargon, repetitive language, inflated claims, and concrete signs of reader friction. Calibrate tone and formatting to the publication and any supplied style guide. Flag a specific phrase and explain the effect; no phrase, punctuation mark, or sentence pattern proves AI authorship. An em-dash ban, prohibited-word list, or other house style applies only when the user provides it. When checking a supplied ban, distinguish literal matches from judgment calls.

## 5. Evidence and attribution

Identify claims that need support and the evidence appropriate to the reader. Classify relevant claims as **verified against an accessible source**, **source cited but not checked**, **unsupported**, or **not verifiable with the available material**. A source link by itself is not verification. Do not invent citations, results, quotes, permissions, or customer examples. A quote must match its accessible source exactly. Mark unresolved factual claims `[NEED: proof]` and uncertain permission or attribution `[Attribution TBD]`. For published material, do not treat an internally sourced claim as publicly reusable without confirmation.

## 6. Travel and distribution

Use the single test declared for the piece. For skim-oriented pieces, extract the visible surface verbatim: title, headings, emphasis, lists, captions, and call to action where present. Report what that surface communicates and where it loses the reader. Do not invent headings or bold text. An outline, a tutorial, a blog, and a report need different travel checks. Assess distribution or search intent only when relevant. No claim of SEO performance follows from an editorial review alone.

## Finding severity and decision ownership

- **Must-fix:** blocks the stated purpose or creates an important factual, attribution, or other publication risk. Name the applicable rule and why it blocks readiness.
- **Should-fix:** materially improves the piece but is not a readiness blocker.
- **Optional:** plausible preference or alternative without evidence that the current choice fails.
- **Escalation:** not a severity. A decision that the author cannot make alone, such as an unconfirmed strategic angle, sensitive attribution, or a change in scope. Phrase it as a question for the relevant owner; say if the owner is unknown. A must-fix can also need escalation.

An absence finding cites the relevant section and says what was expected; do not fabricate a missing quote. A direct quote must appear verbatim in the draft. If a finding depends on an unconfirmed audience or intended use, label it PROVISIONAL. A potential blocker may be labeled PROVISIONAL Must-fix when it would block the stated assumption; explain which assumption must be confirmed before its severity is final.

## Readiness

A positive publication-readiness assessment needs the relevant audience and intended use confirmed, required review lenses complete, no unresolved must-fix, the declared travel test passed or explicitly not applicable, and factual or attribution risks resolved for the intended use. A negative assessment such as Revise is appropriate while blockers remain. Open `[NEED: proof]` or `[Attribution TBD]` tags remain blockers when the claim or attribution will appear in a published piece. Listing an unresolved tag alone does not clear it. If any gate cannot be assessed, say so. Human approval, legal review, and fact-checking remain separate where needed.
